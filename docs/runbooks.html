9. Runbooks

Procedures for the operations the panel does not do by itself. Each one names the signals that call for it, the preconditions, the exact requests and commands, how to verify the result and how to go back - and ends with the codes it produces or reads. Where the product offers no route or command for a step, the runbook says so rather than describing one.

They live in docs/runbooks/ of the repository and are written to be followed at three in the morning by somebody who did not write them.

Installing from nothing

Reach for it when there is no installation yet: the control plane from its image, the first administrator, the backup pair before the fleet exists, and the first hosts from signed packages. It is the short form of chapter 3, and the one to hand somebody who is doing this for the first time - it names the three things that are easy to get wrong and cost an hour each (the ownership of the secret files, the SELinux label on them, and pinning a digest in production).

Codes: installation_state_mismatch, gateway_id_in_use, machine_already_enrolled.

CA rotation

Reach for it when flotestro_ca_certificate_expires_in_seconds is getting short, when the setup checklist starts warning thirty days before the fleet CA's end, or when a CA key has to be considered compromised. The rotation is staged so that there is never a moment in which a host distrusts the panel or the panel distrusts a host: a new CA is prepared (trusted, distributed, not signing), then activated (it signs, the old one becomes retired), and the old one leaves the trust set only once no host uses it. There is no push - the bundle travels in enrollment and renewal responses, so the fleet learns the new CA at its own renewal pace, and GET /api/v1/pki reports hosts_missing and ready_to_activate until it has. Every stage is a step-up operation under pki.rotate, held by platform_admin alone.

Codes: incomplete_coverage from the error guide; the PKI routes answer with their own - pki_unavailable, prepare_failed, no_pending_ca, hosts_missing_ca, activate_failed, ca_in_use, reason_required, reauthentication_required.

Database restore

Reach for it to bring the control plane back on a snapshot - in a drill or in earnest - and to settle the work that was in flight when the snapshot was taken. It is also the reference for what the database holds and what does not: the state directory, the environment file, and the signed package repositories are each outside it and each part of the backup set. It carries the cryptographic states a start can stop in (secrets_key_unavailable, issuer_key_unavailable, pki_state_mismatch, crypto_state_ambiguous), the adoption of a pre-existing secret key at the first start after an upgrade, the rotation of the store's key, what can and cannot be recovered when a key is lost, and the check of the audit chain with flotestro-auditverify after the restore.

Read the section on in-flight jobs before the first drill: after a restore to an older point, attempts close as lease_expired, late results settle as superseded_by_result, and an agent that restarted mid-operation answers outcome_unknown.

Queue backlog

Reach for it when work is queued and not starting. It exists to tell apart the four reasons, because the remedies are different and three of them are not a fault at all: a saturated budget, the dispatch rate, a resource lock on the host, or a host that is simply offline. It names the metric for each (flotestro_budget_tokens, flotestro_dispatch_throttled_total, flotestro_resource_lock_wait_seconds, flotestro_job_queue_age_seconds), where the same thing appears in the API (wait_reason on a job, reason_code on a campaign target), how to raise a budget under If-Match, and how to pause or cancel a campaign. A campaign stop never interrupts a host: the remedies here change what starts next.

Codes: budget_capacity, budget_fair_share, resource_busy, offline, offline_deadline, session_stale, session_unowned, session_fence_stale, precondition_failed (412, on an If-Match write).

Relay buffer full, relay disk full

Reach for it when a site's relay reports its buffer above 70 %, when /readyz answers relay_spool_critical or relay_spool_unwritable, or when a site has been cut off long enough that what it is holding matters. It separates the two resources that can run out - the in-memory buffer and the durable spool on disk - says exactly what is dropped first and what is protected by the critical reserve, and gives the relay's own tool, flotestro-relayctl, for the parts the panel cannot see. It also covers re-enrolling a relay whose identity was lost or compromised.

Read the note on /healthz before touching a relay in an outage: liveness stays 200 through an outage of the centre on purpose, because a relay whose link is down is the one process that must not be restarted. /readyz carries safe_to_restart, and it is true only when nothing is waiting in the spool.

Samples stop arriving, or a chart has a hole

Reach for it whenever a host's chart stops or shows a gap - and take it seriously, because the panel is the only alerting channel of this product: a host whose samples stop is a host whose alert rules cannot fire. The runbook tells apart the five causes and treats each one: the host is away, the agent cannot spool, the panel refuses the samples as too late, the rollup has stopped, or the alert evaluator is running nowhere. The signals are all in one place - the monitoring block of GET /api/v1/status, with raw_partitions, dirty_buckets, evaluator_holder and the retentions the rest has to be read against.

Codes: metric_sample_too_old, metric_sample_not_kept, alert_evaluator_lease_lost, metrics_retention_too_short.

Quarantine, identity recovery and decommission

Reach for it to cut a host off from the fleet, to bring it back, to replace its identity, or to retire it. The lifecycle states are active, quarantined, recovery, retiring and retired, and a transition the state machine does not allow is refused with 409 lifecycle_conflict. All four routes are step-up operations with a reason of at least eight characters and the typed hostname. The runbook also explains what the clone policy does when one certificate is alive on two boots at once, and how a decommission is handed over when the instance that owns the host's session is not the one that took the decision.

Codes: host_quarantined, lifecycle_conflict, lifecycle_quarantined, identity_mismatch, revoked_certificate, remote_cleanup_unconfirmed, lifecycle_handover_pending.

Support bundle

Reach for it when flotestro-agentctl diagnose alone does not settle the question - a host that will not connect, a helper that does not answer, a renewal that keeps failing - or when somebody asks for "the logs". Send the bundle instead of loose files, because the bundle is the form that was scanned. It is made on the host, by an explicit command, into a file the operator names and can read before sending it anywhere; there is no route that pulls one off a host, deliberately. Every collector declares each field as public, sensitive or secret: the public and the sensitive travel, the secret is never collected, and the manifest says what was left out and why.

Codes: bundle_private_key_found, bundle_bearer_token_found, bundle_password_in_url_found and bundle_enrollment_token_found refuse a bundle outright; bundle_file_unparsable withholds one file; bundle_field_not_collected, bundle_field_redacted and bundle_field_pattern_only say in the manifest which layer kept a field out.

Golden image and cloud-init

Reach for it before building a machine image that carries the agent, and before turning a snapshot of a running host into a template. The rule it exists to enforce is that an image must carry no identity: the host row is keyed by /etc/machine-id, the identity lives under identity/current, and a session is recognised by a certificate fingerprint - so an image that carries any of the three produces a fleet in which many machines are one host. It lists what may and must not be in an image, the sanitisation before sealing (including the firewall registry, which would otherwise put one clone's rules on every machine made from it), and the first-boot enrollment through flotestro-enroll.service, which runs only while identity/current/agent.pem does not exist.

Codes: duplicate_machine_id, machine_id_mismatch, machine_id_retired, enrollment_token_invalid, machine_already_enrolled, lifecycle_quarantined; the audit action security.duplicate_identity.

And the checks a change passes before it is released

What every push and every tag has to survive - the tests, the linters, the vulnerability scan, the fuzz targets, the Compose parse checks and the image build for both platforms - and what to do when one of them fails, is in CONTRIBUTING.md. It is not a runbook for an operator; it is the one for whoever is releasing.
